## 1. METR
A. https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/ (METR, July 10, 2025); arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089 ("Submitted on 12 Jul 2025 (v1), last revised 25 Jul 2025").
- CONFIRMED (blog): "When developers are allowed to use AI tools, they take 19% longer to complete issues—a significant slowdown that goes against developer beliefs and expert forecasts."
- CONFIRMED (blog): "developers expected AI to speed them up by 24%, and even after experiencing the slowdown, they still believed AI had sped them up by 20%."
- CONFIRMED (arXiv HTML): "we find that when AI is allowed, developers spend approximately 9% of their time reviewing and cleaning AI generated outputs when working with AI."
- CONFIRMED (arXiv HTML): "When using Cursor, developers accept <<44% of the generations." (raw HTML "&lt;&lt;44%", LaTeX \ll).
- Expert forecasts 39% (economics) / 38% (ML) shorter: CONFIRMED. 143 hours of labelled recordings: CONFIRMED.
B. https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/ (METR, February 24, 2026).
- CONFIRMED: "we believe that the data from our new experiment gives us an unreliable signal of the current productivity effect of AI tools."
- CONFIRMED: "For the subset of the original developers who participated in the later study, we now estimate a speedup of -18% with a confidence interval between -38% and +9%. Among newly-recruited developers the estimated speedup is -4%, with a confidence interval between -15% and +9%."
- CONFIRMED: "we believe it is likely that developers are more sped up from AI tools now — in early 2026 — compared to our estimates from early 2025."
- Caveat: selection effects; estimate likely a lower bound. Redesign options: developer-level randomization, shorter intensive experiments, fixed-task experiments (CONFIRMED).

## 4. Other quantitative
- LinearB https://linearb.io/resources/software-engineering-benchmarks-report (undated; "8.1+ million pull requests from 4,800+ organizations"). CONFIRMED: "AI PRs wait 4.6x longer before review – but are reviewed 2x faster once picked up." CONFIRMED: "Acceptance Rates for AI-generated PRs are significantly lower than manual PRs (32.7% vs. 84.4%)." "17.6 hours vs 3.4" and "5.3x": SNIPPET ONLY.
- Sonar https://www.sonarsource.com/company/press-releases/sonar-data-reveals-critical-verification-gap-in-ai-coding/ (January 8, 2026; "over 1,100 developers"). CONFIRMED: "while 96% of developers report they do not fully trust that AI-generated code is functionally correct, only 48% state they always check their AI-assisted code before committing it." CONFIRMED: "the surge in output has created a new bottleneck at the verification stage". CONFIRMED: "38% of developers noting that reviewing AI-generated code requires more effort than reviewing code written by their human colleagues."
- Stack Overflow 2025 https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai (undated). CONFIRMED: "More developers actively distrust the accuracy of AI tools (46%) than trust it (33%), and only a fraction (3%) report "highly trusting" the output." ## 5. Comprehension/cognitive debt, over-trust
- Duma et al. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02273 (Submitted on 4 May 2026). CONFIRMED (abstract): "most AI-generated PRs receive no review and, when reviewed, are largely dominated by AI agents rather than humans." CONFIRMED (HTML): "61.38% (20,621) receive no recorded review activity" of 33,596 agent PRs; 71.58% of review comments on agent PRs AI-written (table). Caveat: same-repo subset, agent PRs unreviewed 28.92% vs human 34.52%.
- Perry et al. https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03622 (7 Nov 2022). CONFIRMED: "participants with access to an AI assistant were more likely to believe they wrote secure code than those without access"; "wrote significantly less secure code".
- Anthropic https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills (Jan 29, 2026; arXiv 2601.20245, 28 Jan 2026; 52 engineers). CONFIRMED: "the AI group averaged 50% on the quiz, compared to 67% in the hand-coding group"; "participants in the AI group scored 17% lower"; "The largest gap in scores between the two groups was on debugging questions".
- Storey https://margaretstorey.com/blog/2026/02/09/cognitive-debt/ (2026-02-09 per URL). CONFIRMED: "the debt compounded from going fast lives in the brains of the developers"; "no one on the team could explain why certain design decisions had been made". ACM Queue version (queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=3807966) not fetched.
- Osmani https://addyosmani.com/blog/comprehension-debt/ (March 14, 2026). CONFIRMED: "Comprehension debt is the growing gap between how much code exists in your system and how much of it any human being genuinely understands."; "a junior engineer can now generate code faster than a senior engineer can critically audit it."
- MIT arXiv 2506.08872 (10 Jun 2025): title only CONFIRMED ("Accumulation of Cognitive Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay Writing Task"); essay writing, not code.

Not found: a primary study measuring human "LGTM" rubber-stamping of AI PRs specifically (Duma et al. closest); no Microsoft-specific RCT fetched.
